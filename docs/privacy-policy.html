InkPrint Privacy Policy

Effective 22 September 2026 · Last updated 7 October 2026

English · 中文

InkPrint does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no servers. Everything it handles stays on your own device and your own local network.

What InkPrint does

InkPrint turns an Android device — typically an e-ink reader — into a virtual network printer. Other devices on the same Wi-Fi network discover it over Bonjour/mDNS and send documents to it using the standard IPP printing protocol. Each document is saved on the device as a PDF for reading. InkPrint never prints to paper and never forwards a document anywhere.

Information we collect

None. The developer operates no backend service and receives no data from the app. InkPrint contains no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising, or tracking libraries of any kind, and it makes no network connections to the developer or to any third party.

Information processed on your device

The following is handled entirely on your device to make printing work. It is never sent off the device by InkPrint.

WhatWhyWhere it goes
The documents you print They are the thing being printed Saved as PDF files on the device. By default they are kept in the app's own folder and copied to the shared Documents/InkPrint/ folder so your reader app can open them. If you choose a save folder in the app, documents are moved into that folder instead; InkPrint only gets access to the folder you pick, through the system folder picker. They remain on the device until you delete them.
Document titles Used to name the saved file so you can recognise it Stored on the device as part of the file name.
The user name reported by the sending computer, and the job title Required by the IPP protocol to answer a sender's "what is my job status?" query Held in memory only, for the duration of the job. Never written to storage, and discarded when the printer service stops.
Your device's network IP addresses (Wi-Fi/Ethernet, and Tailscale or VPN if active) A printer has to announce an address for other devices to reach it Shown in the app so you know where to print. Only the local network address is broadcast, over mDNS, on your local network. These are private addresses that are not reachable from the public internet.

Network behaviour

InkPrint listens for print jobs on your local network and announces itself over mDNS on that same network. If your device is also on a private network such as Tailscale or a VPN, devices on that network can reach the printer too. InkPrint never initiates a connection: it does not connect to the internet, and it has no remote endpoint to connect to. The INTERNET permission Android requires is used solely to open a listening socket.

Sharing

InkPrint does not share data with anyone. There are no third-party SDKs, no advertising partners, and no data processors, because no data ever leaves your device.

Retention and deletion

Received documents stay on your device until you remove them. You can delete them with any file manager, or remove all of them at once by uninstalling the app — note that files in the shared Documents/InkPrint/ folder, or in a save folder you chose, survive uninstallation and can be deleted manually. InkPrint also lets you rename, move and delete received documents in the app.

Security

Please be aware of how InkPrint works so you can decide where to use it:

  • Like most consumer network printers, InkPrint accepts print jobs from any device on the same local network (or on a Tailscale/VPN network your device is part of) without a password. Use it on networks you trust, and stop the service when you are not using it.
  • Print traffic on the local network is not encrypted, which is normal for IPP printing but means it is only as private as the network you are on.
  • Received documents are stored unencrypted on the device, in a shared folder (or the save folder you chose) that other apps with storage access can read.

Children

InkPrint is a general-purpose utility. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age.
